# ECC in deep-learning image watermarking systems (2018–2026)

Method note: most facts below were checked against the full text of the papers (arXiv PDFs, latest version as of 2026-11-04, searched for "error correct", BCH, Reed–Solomon, ECC, channel coding, LDPC, polar, repetition, majority vote, word accuracy, capacity, Shannon) and, where noted, against the official code (shallow clones of adobe/trustmark, microsoft/InvisMark, facebookresearch/videoseal at commit 870ca7f of 2026-07-02, XuandongZhao/PRC-Watermark; raw tancik/StegaStamp/encode_image.py). "Not mentioned" means the keyword search of the full text found nothing, which does not prove a footnote or supplement elsewhere is silent.

## Q1. Per-system inventory: does each learned watermark use an ECC, which code, payload before/after coding, and is exact-message recovery reported?

### Takeaway
Only a minority of neural image watermarks ship an ECC, and all of those use a short binary BCH code on a payload of about 100–256 bits: StegaStamp, TrustMark's code release, InvisMark, and RoSteALS's evaluation. Every Meta model (SSL watermarking, Stable Signature, WAM, Video Seal, Pixel Seal, ChunkySeal, AudioSeal) and most academic models (HiDDeN, RivaGAN, MBRS, CIN, VINE, EditGuard, AquaLoRA) use no ECC and report bit accuracy, sometimes alongside a p-value or TPR at a fixed FPR. Many of them build in "learned redundancy" or spatial repetition instead. The only modern codes (LDPC with belief propagation (BP), polar, Reed–Solomon (RS), BCH+Chase) appear in diffusion-latent watermarks (PRC, Gaussian Shading++) and in system papers built around existing models (DREW, QRMark).

**Early end-to-end models (2018–2022)**
- HiDDeN trains its watermarking models with message length L = 30 on 3×128×128 images; its steganography models hide 52 bits per 16×16 patch (0.203 BPP) with an error rate < 10⁻⁵, and the authors write "One can use error correcting codes if higher accuracy is desired." No ECC is used. — [HiDDeN, arXiv 1807.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09937)
- ReDMark embeds 1024-bit (32×32) watermarks in 512×512 grayscale images "with four times redundancy": a 64×64 "redundant plane" in which "each bit of watermark is repeated four times in a regular pattern" (in effect a rate-1/4 repetition code); no algebraic ECC. — [ReDMark, arXiv 1810.07248](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.07248)
- StegaStamp: "we settle on a message length of 100 bits… Given an estimate of at least 95% recovery accuracy, we can encode at least 56 error corrected bits using BCH codes"; "The transmitted data consists of 56 message bits and 40 error correcting bits. When the accuracy is greater than 95% (fewer than 5 corrupted bits), the original 56-bit message can be recovered exactly. If too many bits are corrupted, the error correcting fails and we filter out the proposal." The paper also converts mean bit accuracy into BSC capacity C(p) = 1 − h(p) to compare methods. — [StegaStamp, arXiv 1904.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05343)
- StegaStamp code: `bchlib.BCH(BCH_POLYNOMIAL=137, BCH_BITS=5)`, a 7-byte (56-bit) secret, `packet = data + ecc` (5 ECC bytes = 40 bits), then `secret.extend([0,0,0,0])` → 100 bits; it prints "Can only encode 56bits (7 characters) with ECC". — [StegaStamp encode_image.py](https://github.com/tancik/StegaStamp/blob/master/encode_image.py)
- RivaGAN (video, 32 bits): "the receiver is able to decode the 32-bit watermark with above 95% accuracy in all cases. We note that this low error rate can easily be compensated for through error correcting codes". No ECC is used; its "Hamming vector pairs" are a training trick (message and its complement in the same batch), not a code. — [RivaGAN, arXiv 1909.01285](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01285)
- Distortion Agnostic Deep Watermarking (Luo, Zhan, Chang, Yang, Milanfar, CVPR 2020) is the first deep-watermarking paper to make channel coding a core component. Robustness "comes from two sources: adversarial training and channel coding." D = 30 message bits are expanded to N = 120 coded bits by NECST, a learned joint source-channel code trained on a binary symmetric channel (BSC) with noise p ~ U[0, 0.3]. LDPC was rejected because "traditional codes such as LDPC require the decoder to have an estimate of the channel noise strength, which is impractical… since the noise strength can vary greatly from image to image." The code is deliberately "not jointly trained with the rest of the watermarking model" because co-adaptation "results in overfitting and less robustness." — [Luo et al., arXiv 2001.04580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04580)
- MBRS uses a learned "message processor" to "auto-learn a better way to expand the secret messages and achieve redundancy", plus a "diffusion block" for crop robustness. It reports BER (< 0.01% under JPEG Q=50) and uses no ECC. Lengths used include L = 64 for 128×128 and L = 625 for 400×400 images. — [MBRS, arXiv 2108.08211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08211). WAM's appendix notes that the public MBRS model "has a 256-bit payload". — [WAM, arXiv 2411.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07231)
- CIN replicates the watermark and uses "different fully connected (FC) branches [that] produce redundant watermarks of longer length", again learned redundancy rather than an ECC. CIN "only handles 30 bits" (per WAM). — [CIN, arXiv 2212.12678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12678); [WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07231)
- SSL watermarking (Fernandez et al., 2022) uses 30 bits at PSNR 40 dB and is one of the few papers to report word error rate (WER) next to BER. Examples: Identity BER 0.1% / WER 0.7%; Crop 0.5 BER 4.8% / WER 58.3%; JPEG 50 BER 20.8% / WER 98.9%; Screenshot BER 23.9% / WER 100%. The authors add: "In practice, ECC can be used to achieve lower WERs." — [SSL watermarking, arXiv 2112.09581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09581)

**BCH-coded systems (2023–2025)**
- TrustMark paper (Adobe): 100-bit payload. "a bit errors can be alleviated using a forward-error correction method such as BCH at the cost of shortening effective payload in the watermark. **We do not apply such correction in any of our reported results.**" All results are bit accuracy, e.g. TrustMark (α_max = 15) PSNR 38.87 with noised accuracy 0.95. — [TrustMark, arXiv 2311.18297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18297)
- TrustMark code (adobe/trustmark): "TrustMark encodes a payload… of 100 bits. You can configure an error correction level over the raw 100 bits." Four version bits are used, two of them for the schema: 0 BCH_SUPER, 1 BCH_5, 2 BCH_4, 3 BCH_3. In `datalayer.py`, `BCH_POLYNOMIAL = 137`; BCH_SUPER uses t = 8 with 40 data bits, BCH_5 t = 5 with 61, BCH_4 t = 4 with 68, BCH_3 t = 3 with 75. The FAQ says "By default, BCH error correction is applied… user-selectable at the time of encoding and auto-detected at decoding." — [adobe/trustmark README](https://github.com/adobe/trustmark), [FAQ](https://github.com/adobe/trustmark/blob/main/FAQ.md), [datalayer.py](https://github.com/adobe/trustmark/blob/main/python/trustmark/datalayer.py)
- InvisMark (Microsoft, Xu et al.): "we demonstrate the successful encoding of 256-bit watermarks… This enables the embedding of UUIDs with error correction codes, achieving near-perfect decoding success rates." It introduces the "watermark decoding success rate… the probability of complete watermark recovery with error corrections… vital for content provenance applications, where accurate decoding of all bits is essential for database retrieval." Each 256-bit watermark carries a "128-bit UUID and error correction bits". Reported bit accuracy / success rate: DIV2K JPEG 99.3 / 98.9, DALL·E 3 JPEG 97.7 / 92.7, DIV2K RandomResizedCrop 97.9 / 95.2, clean 100 / 100, at PSNR 47.9 dB. — [InvisMark, arXiv 2411.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07795)
- InvisMark code: `configs.py` sets `enc_mode = "ecc"`, `ecc_t = 16`, `ecc_m = 8`; `model.py` wraps `bchlib.BCH(t, m=m)`; `train.py` asserts "Encode 256 bits in ecc mode" and decodes with `bch.decode` / `bch.correct`. — [microsoft/InvisMark](https://github.com/microsoft/InvisMark)
- RoSteALS (Bui et al., CVPRW 2023; 100-bit secret) reports "Bit acc. (ECC) (bit accuracy with cyclic error correction code using BCH, following the settings in [StegaStamp])" and "Word acc." (match if < 20% of bits differ). On CLIC (bit acc. / Bit acc. (ECC) / Word acc.): RoSteALS 0.94 / 1.00 / 0.93; StegaStamp 0.88 / 0.48±0.50 / 0.74; RivaGAN 0.77 / 0.22±0.41 / 0.45; SSL 0.62 / 0.03±0.17 / 0.13; dwtDctSvd 0.61 / 0.16±0.34 / 0.21. The authors note "a side effect of error correction". — [RoSteALS, arXiv 2304.03400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03400)

**Latent / diffusion watermarks**
- Stable Signature: 48-bit signatures; detection and identification use a statistical test on the number of matching bits (FPR from a regularized incomplete beta function). The full text never mentions error correction. — [Stable Signature, arXiv 2303.15435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15435)
- Tree-Ring and RingID are key-pattern (zero-bit or multi-key identification) schemes with no ECC. — [Tree-Ring, arXiv 2305.20030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20030); [RingID, arXiv 2404.14055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14055)
- Gaussian Shading reaches 256 bits by repetition: the watermark is replicated f_c·f_hw² times in the latent, and at extraction, "Similar to voting, if the bit is set to 1 in more than half of the copies, the corresponding watermark bit is set to 1". This is a repetition code with hard majority decoding. Reported "bit accuracy can exceed… 0.97" under noise. — [Gaussian Shading, arXiv 2404.04956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04956)
- PRC watermark (Gunn, Zhao, Song, ICLR 2025): initial latents are chosen with a "pseudorandom error-correcting code" (Christ & Gunn 2024). The authors "use the LDPC construction from Christ & Gunn (2024), modified to handle soft decisions… random t-sparse parity checks". Messages are decoded with "belief propagation with ordered statistics decoding". "We can robustly encode 512 bits… and up to 2500 bits when the images are not subjected to watermark removal attacks." The decoder "is slower and less robust than the detector". The code depends on `ldpc==0.1.51` (`bp_decoder`) and `galois`. — [PRC watermark, arXiv 2410.07369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07369); [PRC-Watermark repo](https://github.com/XuandongZhao/PRC-Watermark)
- AquaLoRA (48 bits) and Artificial Fingerprinting (Yu et al., ICCV 2021; soft matching via a null-hypothesis test on matching bits) use no ECC. — [AquaLoRA, arXiv 2405.11135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11135); [Artificial Fingerprinting, arXiv 2007.08457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08457)

**Other 2024–2025 post-hoc models**
- VINE (ICLR 2025, 100-bit decoder): "all reported baseline bit accuracies do not include error correction methods, such as BCH… which can be applied to all watermarking models." — [VINE, arXiv 2410.18775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18775)
- EditGuard embeds "a 64-bit copyright watermark" with no ECC. — [EditGuard, arXiv 2312.08883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08883)

### Inferences
- The StegaStamp and TrustMark BCH codes are the same family: a binary BCH over GF(2^7) (primitive polynomial 137 = x⁷+x³+1, n = 127, shortened) with ≈7t parity bits. That fits TrustMark's numbers exactly: 40+56 = 61+35 = 68+28 = 75+21 = 96 bits plus 4 version bits. StegaStamp's 40 "ECC bits" are 35 parity bits rounded up to 5 bytes by bchlib. Code rates over the 100 embedded bits are therefore 0.40 to 0.75, correcting at most 3–8 errors (3–8% raw BER).
- InvisMark's code settings imply BCH over GF(2^8) (n = 255, shortened), t = 16, with 16 parity bytes = 128 bits and 16 data bytes = 128 bits. That is a rate-1/2 code correcting ≤ 16 errors (6.25% BER) in a 256-bit block. This matches the paper's "128-bit UUID and error correction bits". This is derived from bchlib semantics, not stated in the paper.
- Across systems with an ECC, the code is always hard-decision bounded-distance BCH, even though every neural extractor outputs soft values (Pixel Seal's extractor "outputs a 'soft' message m̃ ∈ [0,1]^nbits which is thresholded"; [Pixel Seal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16874)). No post-hoc neural image watermark found here feeds extractor logits to an LDPC, polar, or turbo decoder. In that sense the requester's setup (5G LDPC, polar, or turbo with soft inputs on ChunkySeal or Pixel Seal) is not established practice.
- The bimodal "Bit acc. (ECC) 0.48 ± 0.50" for StegaStamp in RoSteALS shows the BCH cliff: images are either decoded perfectly or fail. Codes with bounded-distance decoding turn a 0.88 mean bit accuracy into roughly coin-flip exact recovery when the per-image error count straddles t.

### Gaps
- TrustMark's ICCV 2025 camera-ready ("Robust Watermarking and Watermark Removal for Arbitrary Resolution Images") was not read. Only arXiv 2311.18297 was, so it is unknown whether the final version reports BCH-decoded success rates.
- The RivaGAN and MBRS code repositories were not inspected for optional ECC wrappers.
- Pixel Seal's comparison describes InvisMark as carrying "94 bits", while the InvisMark paper reports 100- and 256-bit models. The reason (e.g., a released checkpoint) was not resolved.
- Exact CIN and MBRS default payloads were taken partly from WAM's description (30 and 256 bits) and not re-checked in the original code.

## Q2. Papers that explicitly study ECC on top of neural (or learned/latent) watermarks: ablations, code comparisons, soft decoding, learned vs algebraic, joint training

### Takeaway
Explicit ECC studies exist but are few and narrow. Luo et al. 2020 is the only learned-channel-code ablation found for an end-to-end neural watermark, and it decoupled the code from training on purpose. Most quantitative "ECC vs no-ECC" or "code A vs code B" evidence comes from diffusion-latent schemes: Gaussian Shading++ (PRC/LDPC vs BCH, soft vs hard decoding) and the 2026 latent sign-clamping paper (BCH vs BCH+Chase). The rest comes from system wrappers around TrustMark or Stable Signature: DREW (polar codes) and QRMark (Reed–Solomon). No study was found that compares LDPC, polar, turbo, BCH, and RS with soft extractor LLRs on a post-hoc neural watermark like Video Seal, Pixel Seal, or ChunkySeal. No image watermark found trains with a differentiable algebraic decoder in the loop.

### Cited Findings
- **Luo et al. 2020 ablation (bit accuracy, %)**. Columns: Identity / Channel coding only / Adversarial training only / Both. JPEG Q=50: 50.2 / 51.3 / 85.0 / 81.7. Crop p=0.09: 53.0 / 60.5 / 90.6 / 93.5. Blur σ=1: 59.6 / 50.2 / 86.2 / 92.8. Dropout p=0.3: 81.3 / 90.3 / 95.0 / 97.9. The authors conclude that "channel coding alone does not provide enough robustness without a robust watermarking model. However, combined with adversarial training channel coding further boosts the performance… especially if the bit accuracy is already high." For adversarial-only models, the input length is 30 instead of 120. Their Figure 6 sweeps the NECST code length N = 90–150 for D = 30 on a BSC. — [Luo et al., arXiv 2001.04580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04580)
- **Gaussian Shading++ (2025)** uses a pseudorandom LDPC-based code to carry a random-seed header, decoded with BP-OSD, plus "a novel soft decision decoding strategy". Generation and inversion are modelled as an AWGN channel, and "watermark recovery [is reformulated] as a maximum likelihood decoding problem of repetition codes", with per-copy posterior expectations aggregated into a per-bit LLR. The ablation (Table VII, TPR @ FPR 10⁻⁶ / bit accuracy, SD 2.1) replaces PRC with BCH and finds a "notable decline". For example, BCH+SDD gets JPEG 0.588 / 0.794, GauBlur 0.008 / 0.503 and MedFilter 0.062 / 0.531, where PRC+SDD gets 0.974 / 0.984, 0.974 / 0.972 and 0.964 / 0.974. Soft vs hard decoding gives smaller gains: PRC+HDD JPEG 0.966 / 0.977 and GauBlur 0.970 / 0.948, against PRC+SDD 0.974 / 0.984 and 0.974 / 0.972. — [Gaussian Shading++, arXiv 2504.15026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15026)
- **Latent Coordinate Sign Clamping (arXiv 2610.40204, 2026, training-free diffusion-latent)**. A 32-bit user ID is "BCH(63,36,t=5)-encoded into 63 coded bits, each repeated 5 times", over m = 317 latent positions. Decoding has three layers: magnitude-weighted (soft) majority vote, then BCH, then Chase decoding (d = 7). Chase "boosts extraction accuracy from 81.8% to 91.7%" on the hardest attack (Cheng20). Per dataset: COCO 74.8 → 89.4, DiffDB 86.2 → 93.4, WikiArt 84.4 → 92.2, by "trial-flipping unreliable coded bits before BCH correction, rescuing images with 6–12 coded-bit errors". It reports a 94.6% decode rate against CtrlRegen+ regeneration. — [arXiv 2610.40204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40204)
- **DREW (Saberi et al., 2024)** wraps TrustMark (100 bits; StegaStamp in the appendix) with "Polar Codes… with a successive cancellation decoder. The reliability of decoding is checked using the last log-likelihood ratio value, with a threshold set at 0.5." When the code is unreliable, it falls back to whole-dataset retrieval. The paper reports retrieval-accuracy gains "up to 40% for some datasets and modification types". It also argues that "the volume of information that can be reliably embedded as a watermark is limited (e.g., 100–200 bits), restricting the inclusion of sufficient redundant bits for effective error correction." — [DREW, arXiv 2406.02836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02836)
- **QRMark (2025)** decodes Stable Signature (48-bit) from image tiles for speed. Tile decoding alone drops bit accuracy from 99.7% to 87.5%. RS over 8-bit symbols with m_c = 2 redundant symbols ("correction of up to one erroneous symbol") and a Berlekamp–Welch decoder is used to recover accuracy, reaching for example bit accuracy 0.989–0.999 at tile sizes 32–64. The system speedup is 2.43×. — [QRMark, arXiv 2509.02447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02447)
- **RoSteALS** gives a no-ECC vs BCH comparison across six methods in one table (see Q1). Its RoSteALS model goes from 0.94 bit accuracy to 1.00 "Bit acc. (ECC)", while weaker models collapse under BCH (StegaStamp 0.88 → 0.48, SSL 0.62 → 0.03). — [RoSteALS, arXiv 2304.03400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03400)
- **InvisMark** reports BCH decoding success rate vs raw bit accuracy (Table 6; see Q1), for example 97.7% bit accuracy → 92.7% exact UUID recovery on DALL·E 3 under JPEG. — [InvisMark, arXiv 2411.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07795)
- **WAM's soft repetition decoding of a baseline.** To compare against MBRS (256 bits), WAM's authors "encode this 16+32 = 48-bit message 5 times, occupying 240 bits. At decoding time, we average the outputs of the decoder every 48 bits (soft majority vote)." This is an ad hoc rate-1/5 repetition code with soft combining. — [WAM, arXiv 2411.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07231)
- **Other ECC + neural-extractor work found via search (not read in full):** "Watermarking for Social Networks Images With Improved Robustness Through Polar Codes" pairs a neural extractor with polar codes ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383291264_Watermarking_for_social_networks_images_with_improved_robustness_through_polar_codes)). A 2025 Frontiers in Physics paper reportedly compares LDPC, LDPC+BCH, and BCH in diffusion-model watermarking ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2025.1750515/full)). An older "Implementation of BCH coding on artificial neural network-based color image watermarking" also exists ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289749228_Implementation_of_BCH_coding_on_artificial_neural_network-based_color_image_watermarking)).
- **Adjacent (text, not images):** recent LLM-watermark papers argue explicitly for soft decoding. WeaveMark reports that replacing hard voting with soft vote-margin inputs to an ML decoder raises the 32-bit match rate from 18.94% to 80.07% at 200 tokens ([WeaveMark, arXiv 2609.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02177)); CORE-BREW does LLR-based soft decoding ([arXiv 2606.24163](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24163)). Both are known only from search snippets and were not verified.

### Inferences
- Where codes are compared head-to-head on the same watermark channel (Gaussian Shading++), an iterative soft-decodable LDPC-type code clearly beats BCH. Soft decoding adds smaller but consistent gains over hard decoding. Chase, a soft-assisted add-on for BCH, adds about 10 points of exact-decode rate on hard attacks. This is the strongest published evidence for the requester's hypothesis that soft LLRs plus modern codes help, but it comes from diffusion-latent watermarks, not post-hoc neural extractors.
- In Luo et al., the JPEG cell where "Both" (81.7) is below "Adv." (85.0) is consistent with a rate-1/4 code that quadruples the bits the network must embed. This trade-off (more raw bits at a fixed perceptual budget means a higher raw BER) is exactly what a fixed-strength (wPSNR 40 dB) ECC comparison must control. Note that the cell compares bit accuracy of the decoded 30 bits against raw 30-bit embedding, so it is a fair end-to-end comparison.
- Joint training with a code in the loop is essentially unexplored for images. Luo et al. tried a learned code and chose to decouple it to avoid overfitting. No differentiable BCH, LDPC, or polar decoder inside a watermark training loop was found.

### Gaps
- No paper found evaluates 5G LDPC, polar (SCL/CRC-aided), turbo, BCH, RS, and repetition side by side on a post-hoc neural image watermark at fixed image quality. No paper found uses extractor logits as channel LLRs for such codes on Video Seal, Pixel Seal, ChunkySeal, TrustMark, or HiDDeN.
- Semantic Scholar searches were rate-limited (HTTP 429), so the long tail of non-arXiv journal papers (IEEE Access, Elsevier, MDPI) combining CNN watermarks with BCH, RS, or polar codes was not systematically enumerated.
- The Frontiers 2025 BCH/LDPC comparison and the polar-code social-network paper were seen only as search snippets; their numbers are unverified.

## Q3. What Meta's papers (SSL watermarking, Stable Signature, WAM, Video Seal, Pixel Seal, ChunkySeal, AudioSeal) say about ECC, capacity and message recovery

### Takeaway
Meta's watermarking line never uses an ECC. It deliberately evaluates with bit accuracy plus a statistical significance measure: a binomial p-value or TPR at a fixed FPR. Video Seal adds a BSC "expected capacity" C(p) = nbits·(1−h(p)). The only ECC mention is SSL watermarking's remark that "ECC can be used to achieve lower WERs". ChunkySeal (1024 bits) treats capacity in geometric and combinatorial terms: it counts the lattice points (images) admissible under PSNR and linear-robustness constraints and explicitly declines the classical Gel'fand–Pinsker channel-coding framing. It reports only bit accuracy, with no ECC, no exact-match rate, and no coding-rate discussion. The videoseal code base contains no ECC.

### Cited Findings
- SSL watermarking (Fernandez et al. 2022) reports BER and WER for 30 bits and states "In practice, ECC can be used to achieve lower WERs" (see the Q1 numbers). — [arXiv 2112.09581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09581)
- Stable Signature (48 bits) frames detection as a hypothesis test on the number of matching bits and identification as matching against N users' signatures. The full text contains no ECC discussion. — [arXiv 2303.15435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15435)
- WAM (32-bit messages, plus 16 bits for detection in comparisons) does "global decoding, a majority vote recovers the hidden message" over per-pixel predictions. It claims extraction of "distinct 32-bit messages with less than 1 bit error from multiple small regions". In comparisons, "an image is flagged as watermarked if at most 1 bit is wrongly decoded" (16 bits, FPR 2.6×10⁻⁴). — [arXiv 2411.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07231)
- Video Seal (nbits = 96): "The biggest issue with bit accuracy is that it is agnostic to the number of bits being hidden… a method with average bit accuracy p = 0.9 and nbits = 128 has a total capacity bigger than a method with bit accuracy p = 0.99 and nbits = 64, in an information-theoretic sense." It defines "expected capacity" C(p) = nbits × (1 − h(p)), the "number of bits that would be theoretically transmittable from a Shannon perspective, if we assumed that the observed bit accuracy is the true probability of error". It also defines a binomial p-value under H0 that bits are Bernoulli(0.5), and reports log10 p "which takes into account nbits". No ECC is used. — [Video Seal, arXiv 2412.09492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09492)
- Pixel Seal (256 bits) has an extractor that "outputs a 'soft' message m̃ ∈ [0,1]^nbits which is thresholded to recover the binary message". It evaluates "both the bit accuracy and log10 p… This allows for a fair comparison between methods with different payload sizes", with a false-positive bound based on the Hamming-distance binomial tail. Under combined crop+JPEG+brightness attacks, "only Pixel Seal and InvisMark maintain bit accuracy above 90% while Pixel Seal embeds a significantly larger message than InvisMark (256 vs. 94 bits)". No ECC is used. — [Pixel Seal, arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16874)
- ChunkySeal ("We Can Hide More Bits: The Unused Watermarking Capacity in Theory and in Practice", Petrov, Fernandez, Souček, Elsahar) "establishes upper bounds on the message-carrying capacity of images under PSNR and linear robustness constraints". Capacity is the log₂ of "the number of unique such points [images] that do not violate given constraints", computed through ball/cube volumes and exact lattice-point counting (Mitchell 1966). It argues that the Gel'fand–Pinsker capacity I(U;Y) − I(U;S) "cannot be instantiated for real images" because it needs true image densities, while citing Costa 1983 and Moulin & O'Sullivan 2003 for the result that cover knowledge does not change capacity in Gaussian settings. Its bounds give "capacities of over 2 bpp at PSNR of 40 dB without robustness constraints, and of 0.5 bpp with robustness"; the conservative Bound 13 still leaves "at least 904 bits" for 256×256 under the most aggressive crop. — [ChunkySeal, arXiv 2510.12812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12812)
- ChunkySeal experiments: Video Seal trained on a single gray image reaches 100% bit accuracy for 128, 256 and 512 bits but only 89.63% at 1024 bits (40.10 dB). A single linear embedder plus linear extractor reaches "100% bit accuracy for 1024 bits with PSNR of 44 dB" and 100% for 2048 bits, which the authors take as evidence of "structural limitations" of the architecture. ChunkySeal itself is Video Seal with embedding dimension 2048, U-Net multipliers [4,8,16,32], watermarking in all three channels, a 1022.7M-parameter embedder and a 773.7M-parameter extractor. — [arXiv 2510.12812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12812)
- ChunkySeal vs Video Seal 256 on SA-1B (Table 3, bit accuracy): Identity 99.74 ± 0.28% vs 99.90 ± 0.21%; JPEG Q50–80 98.79 ± 0.75% vs 99.74 ± 0.47%; Crop 77–95% 98.25 ± 1.75% vs 98.04 ± 1.57%; Rotate ≤ 10° 98.27 ± 2.10% vs 98.84 ± 1.10%; Brightness 0.5–1.5× 98.99 ± 1.87% vs 98.67 ± 2.67%; Overall 99.15 ± 0.63% vs 99.31 ± 0.60%; PSNR 45.32 vs 44.42 dB. The extended Table 4 shows cliffs: Rotate 30° 49.56%, Crop 32% 49.71%, Crop 55% 86.90%, Brightness 10% 83.62%. ChunkySeal reports only bit accuracy and does not report exact-message recovery. — [arXiv 2510.12812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12812)
- ChunkySeal's discussion: "while ChunkySeal does outperform VideoSeal, it is also much larger and induces significant latencies which make it difficult to deploy at scale or on-device." — [arXiv 2510.12812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12812)
- AudioSeal (16 bits) attributes clips by "returning the identifier with the smallest Hamming distance", a nearest-codeword search over a known set of messages with no ECC. — [AudioSeal, arXiv 2401.17264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17264)
- The facebookresearch/videoseal repository (commit 870ca7f, 2026-07-02), which hosts Video Seal, Pixel Seal and ChunkySeal, contains no matches for bch, reed-solomon, error-correct, ecc or ldpc in its .py/.md/.txt/.toml files. Its README lists "ChunkySeal: 4× capacity increase (1024 bits)" and "PixelSeal" (released December 2025). — [facebookresearch/videoseal](https://github.com/facebookresearch/videoseal)

### Inferences
- Applying Video Seal's own C(p) to ChunkySeal's Table 3 means gives ≈ 1024·(1 − h(0.0026)) ≈ 997 bits for Identity and ≈ 1024·(1 − h(0.0121)) ≈ 927 bits under JPEG Q50–80 (my calculation). On that basis, a rate ~0.5 code carrying 512 bits on 1024 raw bits has a large Shannon margin at average BER. The binding constraint is the per-image and per-attack BER tail (crop, brightness and rotation cliffs) plus burst or correlated errors, not average capacity.
- Without ECC, the probability of exact 1024-bit recovery at ChunkySeal's mean Identity BER (0.26%), assuming i.i.d. errors, is about (0.9974)^1024 ≈ 7% (my calculation). Mean bit accuracies of 99%+ therefore do not imply exact recovery at 1024 bits. This is the gap the requester's ECC layer addresses, and Meta's papers do not measure it.
- Meta's metric choice (p-value / TPR at fixed FPR) fits detection and identification against a known message set, where approximate matching suffices. Exact recovery of an arbitrary 256–512-bit message (e.g., a signature or UUID) is outside the use case their evaluations target. This explains, but does not justify for exact recovery, the absence of ECC.

### Gaps
- No Meta paper was found that runs ECC (or reports word/exact-match accuracy) on Video Seal, Pixel Seal or ChunkySeal. Whether ChunkySeal's per-bit errors are spatially or index-correlated (which matters for interleaving and code choice) is not reported.
- Video Seal's footnote 2 (after the Cover 1999 citation) could not be cleanly extracted from the PDF text.

## Q4. Explicit statements that ECC is unnecessary or harmful, or that bit accuracy is the right (or wrong) metric

### Takeaway
No paper found states that ECC is harmful for neural watermarks. The dominant stance is "ECC is orthogonal and can be added later", so results are reported without it (HiDDeN, RivaGAN, SSL, TrustMark, VINE). Arguments against or around ECC are pragmatic: payload is too small for enough redundancy at large ID spaces (DREW), classical codes need a noise estimate (Luo et al.), a coded message costs raw capacity (Luo, TrustMark). On metrics, Meta argues that bit accuracy alone is inadequate because it ignores nbits, and it adds a p-value or BSC capacity rather than exact-match rates. InvisMark and SSL argue that exact or word-level recovery is what provenance needs.

### Cited Findings
- "One can use error correcting codes if higher accuracy is desired." — [HiDDeN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09937)
- "this low error rate can easily be compensated for through error correcting codes, allowing our system to be used in real-world applications." — [RivaGAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01285)
- "Given the small number of wrongly detected bits, this is of no practical significance as a bit errors can be alleviated using a forward-error correction method such as BCH… at the cost of shortening effective payload… We do not apply such correction in any of our reported results." — [TrustMark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18297)
- "all reported baseline bit accuracies do not include error correction methods, such as BCH, which can be applied to all watermarking models." — [VINE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18775)
- "traditional codes such as LDPC require the decoder to have an estimate of the channel noise strength, which is impractical in our application"; "channel coding alone does not provide enough robustness without a robust watermarking model." — [Luo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04580)
- "the volume of information that can be reliably embedded as a watermark is limited (e.g., 100–200 bits), restricting the inclusion of sufficient redundant bits for effective error correction… with 100-bit watermarks… ECC algorithms cannot provide a highly robust coding" (for billion-scale IDs). — [DREW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02836)
- On metrics: "The biggest issue with bit accuracy is that it is agnostic to the number of bits being hidden"; Video Seal proposes the p-value and C(p) instead. — [Video Seal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09492). Pixel Seal: log10 p "allows for a fair comparison between methods with different payload sizes". — [Pixel Seal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16874)
- Pro-exact-recovery: InvisMark introduces "decoding success rate… vital for content provenance applications, where accurate decoding of all bits is essential for database retrieval". — [InvisMark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07795). SSL watermarking reports WER next to BER and notes that ECC can lower WER. — [SSL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09581). StegaStamp filters out detections where BCH fails. — [StegaStamp](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05343)
- InvisMark's motivation: typical payloads "under 100 bits… elevates the risk of ID collisions in the presence of bit errors". — [InvisMark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07795)

### Inferences
- The field's consensus is implicit: ECC is treated as a solved, separable, application-layer concern (usually "just add BCH"). It is rarely engineered or measured. That leaves soft-decision, capacity-approaching codes on neural extractors at fixed perceptual budget as an open topic rather than established practice. The closest established practice is the BCH-on-100/256-bit pattern of StegaStamp, TrustMark and InvisMark, which the requester's BCH/RS baselines already reproduce.
- The evidence of harm is indirect. (a) In Luo et al.'s JPEG cell, coding lowered bit accuracy (85.0 → 81.7) because the network had to embed 4× more bits. (b) Bounded-distance BCH produces all-or-nothing outcomes (RoSteALS's 0.48 ± 0.50). Both argue for comparing codes at equal image quality and for reporting exact-match rates and residual BER after decoding, which matches the requester's fairness protocol.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that argues bit accuracy is sufficient for exact-message use cases, or that measures whether neural-extractor bit errors are i.i.d. (the assumption behind the BSC C(p) used by StegaStamp and Video Seal). Error-correlation structure in neural extractors is unmeasured in the sources reviewed.
